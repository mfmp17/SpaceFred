date: 2026-11-25
summary: A four-step setup tour that suggests where your open windows go, starts tiling only when you say so, and can undo it.
---
## Major
- Rebuilt the setup tour as four short steps. `Your workspaces` shows the windows you have open as miniature workspaces, with each app's icon where it would tile: browsers on 1, editors and terminals on 2, chat and mail on 3, documents on 4, media on 5, never more than two tiles on a laptop display. Click or drag an icon to move it, then `Arrange & start`.
- Tiling now starts only when you press `Arrange & start`. `Skip` leaves every window where it is, and `Undo & restore` on the last step puts them all back.

## Improvements
- The arrangement moves the windows that are open and saves no app-to-workspace rule, so a browser's or terminal's next window opens where you are. The only rules it writes are `float` rules for small utilities.
- Calculator, Stickies, System Settings, Digital Color Meter, 1Password and Hex start floating instead of taking half a display.
- Settings opens on General. Rules now says what it is: `Where new windows open`, optional, and any window can still move to any workspace.
- An app can have more than one rule. `+` lists every running app, and one that already has a rule asks for a `Title contains` filter, so Safari's Work windows can open on workspace 1 and its Claude windows on workspace 2. Right-click a rule to change its filter.
- Settings › Rules shows the apps Spaceman floats by default under `Floating by default`. Click one to tile it or pin it on all workspaces.
- The Workspace HUD's right-click menu says what each item does: `Always open Safari on Workspace 2` for where new windows open, `Pin Safari on all workspaces` for a float that shows everywhere, and `Tile` for an app that floats by default.
- A `route` rule for an app that floats by default, such as Stickies, now keeps it floating and opens it on the routed workspace instead of tiling it.
- The permission step asks for Accessibility and nothing else. It lists Stage Manager, macOS edge tiling or another window manager only when one is on, with `Turn off` or `Quit`.
- `Try it` is a miniature desktop that teaches the shortcuts one at a time: `⌥2` and `⌥1` to switch workspaces, `⌥⇧Space` to float a window and tile it again, `⌥⇧F` to fill the screen, `⌥1` again to bring the window behind forward, and `⌥⇧F` to come back. The prompt and its keys stay on screen from one lesson to the next, the Workspace HUD stays up to show each move, and the four windows show a little of a real afternoon in Safari, Terminal, Notes and Mail. Each press plays on the miniature, ticks with a soft sound and brings up the next, and none of your real windows move while the setup window has the keyboard. `Modifier` under the prompt changes the modifier every shortcut holds, and `Launch at login` moved here.
- Added `Quit Spaceman` and `Pause & restore windows` to Settings › General, for when the menu bar icon is hidden behind a notch.
- `spaceman ctl move-tab 2` sends the focused window's active tab to a workspace as a window of its own, by pressing the app's own `Move Tab to New Window`, so Safari, Finder, Terminal, Ghostty, Chrome and other tabbed apps need no reload and no extra permission. The remaining tabs stay where they are, the new window goes straight to its target ahead of any `route` rule, and `--follow` switches to it. A window with a single tab moves whole, exactly like `move`. No default shortcut; bind one in `[shortcuts.bindings]`, such as `"ctrl+alt+shift+2" = "move-tab 2"`.
- With Labs › `Swipe switch` on, the three-finger workspace swipe can be turned off, for anyone who swipes between macOS desktops with three fingers: Settings › Shortcuts › `Swipe to switch workspace`, or `trackpad_swipe = false` in `[settings]`. It applies straight away.
- New Lab, `[labs] four_finger_swipe`: the workspace swipe takes four fingers instead of three, for anyone who uses Accessibility's three-finger drag (which moves macOS's own swipes to four fingers). Three-finger swipes are left to macOS. Turn it on in Settings › Labs › Workspaces › Four-finger swipe or with `spaceman config set labs.four_finger_swipe true`.
- New Lab, `[labs] workspace_steps`: `switch next|prev` steps through the focused display's workspaces like a three-finger swipe, and `move next|prev [--follow]` sends the focused window to the next or previous workspace on its display, empty ones included. Bind them under `[shortcuts.bindings]` or in Settings › Shortcuts; none has a built-in chord. Off by default; turn it on in Settings › Labs › Workspaces › Workspace steps or with `spaceman config set labs.workspace_steps true`.
- The Workspace HUD is quicker: it appears about 30% faster, and Icons groups, Layouts cells and scrolling settle 20–30% sooner. The marker keeps its stretch.
- New Lab, `[labs] portrait_edge_rows`: on a display taller than it is wide, drag a tiled window to the top or bottom edge (or into the menu bar) to make it a full-width, half-height row there. Off by default.
- New Lab, `[labs] physical_displays`: the menu bar's workspaces and the menu's display sections follow your desk left to right, a portrait display's glyph and its Workspace HUD Layouts previews are drawn tall, and two displays of the same model are labelled by position, such as `left` and `right`. Off by default; turn it on in Settings › Labs or with `spaceman config set labs.physical_displays true`.
- Settings › Labs lists every Lab with its own switch, all off by default, grouped by what it changes: `Windows`, `Workspaces`, `Menu bar & HUD` and `Settings`. `spaceman://labs/<name>` opens it on one.
- New Lab, `[labs] recent_focus`: when several tiles border the focused one on the side you press, `Option+H/J/K/L` goes to the one you used last, so pressing one way and back returns where you were. Off by default; turn it on in Settings › Labs › Windows › Recent focus or with `spaceman config set labs.recent_focus true`.
- New Lab, `[labs] empty_workspace_focus`: after you show an empty workspace on another display, `⌥⇧Tab` moves that workspace instead of the one whose window kept focus, and `⌥⇧F` and other window commands no longer act on it. It ends when another window takes focus. Off by default. Turn it on in Settings › Labs › Workspaces or with `spaceman config set labs.empty_workspace_focus true`.
- New Lab, `Dragged floats follow their display` (`[labs] float_follows_display`): a floating window dragged to another display joins the workspace showing there, as a tile does, keeping its size and place.

## Fixes
- Activating an app whose window is pinned, by clicking it in the Dock or bringing it back from minimized, no longer switches to the workspace it was pinned from.
- A display that two assignments name (`secondary` and its UUID) keeps showing the same workspace after a restart, an unplug and reconnect, or a config edit that only switches between the two. Moving a workspace back reveals the one that display showed last, and Option+Tab after a restart finds it.
- Floating windows return to their workspace's display when it reconnects, and a tall float carried to a shorter display comes back with its workspace.
- Deleting the only declaration of an unused named workspace makes it unavailable straight away, not after a restart.
- Moving an empty workspace to another display no longer leaves its slot in the old display's HUD.
- Quick keyboard commands no longer lose track of the window just focused while macOS catches up: `focus next`, a numbered move, Option+Tab and the next Dwindle split all start from it, even when the app reports no window or a drop just moved it.
- `ctl debug` and `spaceman hud` show the targets the focus keys really go to in Fullscreen Layout, from a float, from an empty workspace and during quick presses.
- The pointer follows `move-tab --follow` onto the tab's display, goes to the frame a window accepted when it is wider than its tile, and never moves while the right or middle mouse button is held.
- The float shortcut no longer tries to float Spaceman's own Settings window.
- `scratchpad mark` on a summoned window tiles it on the workspace showing it, even when its app has a `route` rule to another workspace.
- `move-tab` to a workspace on another display now brings a floating tab to that display instead of leaving it behind.
- A floating window that reopens at a remembered placement now appears on its remembered workspace's display.
- A new window a `route` rule sends to a hidden workspace no longer stays tiled where it opened when hiding it fails; Spaceman tries again on the next pass.
- Resizing a tile with the pointer only changes its split when you let go. Pausing mid-drag, or a release macOS reports no frame for, puts the other tiles back where they were, and releasing at the same point gives the same widths however the drag went.
- Dragging a tile over its own spot no longer undoes a resize of a temporary stack, and releasing where a swap was previewed swaps even if `config.toml` changed in between.
- A tile dragged off its display in Fullscreen Layout, or moved there by an app, returns to its workspace's display.
- A float released over a side Dock stays where you put it, and a window that opens over the Dock is tiled or floated on that display's workspace.
- With `single_window_max_width` set, focus and move toward a staggered display reach the adjacent one, not the one behind it.
- An emptied named workspace keeps its split direction across a restart.
- A window moved from a landscape display onto a taller portrait one fills its whole tile instead of stopping at the old display's bottom edge.
- `resize width` and `resize height` keep every tile at least 50 points wide past the gaps, keep the sizes windows have refused to go below, apply a change of one or two points, and keep a temporary stack on its display.
- Dwindle splits a tile along the longer side of the frame it shows, and a retile fits a row whose minimum sizes only come to light one window at a time.
- Dragging a window by its title bar onto another display moves it there even when the app changes the window's width as it lands.
- The menu bar icon no longer cuts off the right edge of the last workspace number.
- After a swipe between macOS desktops settles, the arriving desktop's window is no longer tiled next to the one you left, at half width, for about a second.
